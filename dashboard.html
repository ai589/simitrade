How stocks are picked — full methodology
Every strategy runs the same pipeline; only step 3 differs.

1. Universe. ~200 large, liquid NYSE- and Nasdaq-listed names across 7 sectors. Names on other exchanges are dropped automatically; every name must trade ≥ $50M/day (20-day average dollar volume) so entries and exits don't move the price.

2. Common filters. Names reporting earnings within the hold window (7 days for weekly, 14 for the 14-day strategy) are excluded — overnight earnings gaps can jump straight past a stop. Regime-gated strategies additionally require SPY above its 50-day MA (below it for shorts).

3. Selection rule per strategy.
  • Momentum Top-10 / Top-5 / 14-day: keep names in an uptrend (price > 20-day MA > 50-day MA) that aren't overbought (RSI-14 ≤ 75), rank by combined 20-day + 60-day return, take the top N.
  • Low-vol trend: same uptrend requirement, but take the 5 quietest names (lowest 20-day volatility) — trend with the least drama.
  • Sector-neutral momentum: the single highest-momentum name from each sector (~7 picks) — momentum's returns with forced diversification.
  • Weekly dip: among uptrend names, the 5 with the worst return over the last 5 days — buying the routine pullback inside a healthy trend.
  • Value, deep drawdown: the 5 names furthest below their own 52-week high — cheapest vs their own history, no trend filter (the liquid large-cap universe is the safety net).
  • Value, below 200-day MA: names below their 200-day average that have reclaimed their 20-day average — cheap, but already bouncing; ranked cheapest first.
  • Momentum / low-vol z-blend: every name above its 50-day MA gets a momentum z-score (20d+60d return) minus a volatility z-score; take the top 5 — the strongest movers with the least chop. Best Sharpe of all weekly rule sets tested.
  • Short (bear regime only): among names below their 50-day MA, short the sharpest 5-day spike — betting the rally within a downtrend fails. Backtest lost money; kept only as a prepared bear-market tool.
4. Levels. Stop = entry − 1.5×ATR(14), target = entry + 2×ATR (weekly); 2×/3× ATR for the 14-day hold; inverted for shorts. ATR scales the levels to each stock's own volatility.

5. Sizing. Shares = (account × risk%) ÷ stop distance, capped at 20% of account per position and budget ÷ basket size if a budget is set.

6. Exit. Stop hit → out; target hit → out; otherwise sell at the close on the sell-by date. Every pick is also paper-logged automatically and scored against real prices — see the paper-trade record.

7. Weekly rollover. If a name you already hold is picked again this week, keep holding it (tagged ↻ hold) — the backtest's "sell at the close, re-enter at the same close" is economically the same position, so rolling it forward just skips two spreads and commissions. Apply the new week's stop/target to the held position. Names still open that dropped out of the picks appear as orange ✕ sell rows in the same table — those are the ones to actually close this week. The paper-trade record still scores each week independently (backtest-consistent), so its stats stay comparable.
